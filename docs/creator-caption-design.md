# Speech-led caption system

## Reference study

Primary sources: [Apple Music Sing introduction and interface images](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2022/12/apple-introduces-apple-music-sing/) (December 2022), and [Apple's timed-lyrics delivery requirements](https://itunespartner.apple.com/music/support/5319-submitting-content-apple-music-sing) (August 2023). The local reference catalog had no matching Apple Music lyric recording.

The official iPhone image was inspected in the browser, without downloading its media package. It shows left-aligned, substantial-weight type; phrase-based wrapping; a bright foreground phrase; less prominent neighboring phrases; and increasingly softened context toward the viewport edges. The interface is not a series of centered text boxes. Its hierarchy comes from type, opacity, and focus, rather than decorative panels. An ambient background supports the words rather than competing with them.

Apple describes vocal-synchronized animation, independent background vocals, and spatial separation for duet parts. Its delivery documentation requires time-synchronized lyrics for the word/beat-level display. This is important: word-count timers are not equivalent to actual spoken alignment.

The still establishes composition, not exact motion curves. No frame-accurate Apple motion recording was available in this study. The following timing, blur, and distance values are our adaptation, not claims about Apple's implementation. No lyrics, branded intro, or Apple assets are included in rendered videos.

## Mogging adaptation

One native canvas painter owns all captions: `video-captions.ts`. Imported legacy word-build/typewriter/shimmer choices normalize to this typography and reading model, rather than maintaining competing text engines. Ordinary entry/exit tracks still transform the caption layer.

- A 900-pixel column shares a 90-pixel left inset across the 1080-pixel stage. The original bundled Geist Regular face is used at weight 400, without synthetic bold. Line height is 1.16; report and feature-stage captions are 68 pixels, photographic/anatomy captions 72 pixels, and other graphic captions 76 pixels. This intentionally narrow type scale avoids jumps between formats. No score-blue body text, neon shimmer, caret, or random alignment changes.
- Full-screen report captions sit at the bottom in dark ink. A Motion Primitives-style progressive blur enters with the text over 420 ms: five gradient-masked blur levels and a feathered light tint soften the bottom 360 pixels. Three small reusable canvas surfaces per edge/role avoid full-frame blur passes and per-frame allocations. Photographic stages retain top pearl-white captions with a dark progressive backdrop. This is glass-inspired typography, not a physical refraction simulation.
- Complete text determines wrapping before speech begins. Words never push earlier words around. Captions use the actual shot narration, not an unrelated category slogan.
- The focused line is most legible. Neighboring lines sit at 50% layer opacity with up to 0.7 pixels of blur. Spoken words fill from left to right; the unread portion remains visible as context. This replaces words popping into existence.
- Focus transfers over 420 milliseconds using the existing Motion easing adapter. A restrained three-pixel lift gives the active phrase emphasis without bouncing individual letters. Overflow scrolls continuously along the same text column; there is no page-sized jump.
- Photo studies use the original photograph edge-to-edge, not a segmented cutout. Their regular-weight captions are 72 pixels and sit 24 pixels from the top. The brand lockup moves to the bottom over its own shorter 190-pixel progressive blur; both ends share the same painter and cache implementation. Graphics use a bottom brand lockup and layout-owned headline regions. Full-screen reports and supplied app screens use prominent 68-pixel dark captions in a bottom safe area, not a solid header bar or another card.
- White feature stages append the small brand mark to the native metric card beside a short numeric value, except when a grade already occupies that position. Other stages use a bottom footer. The shared brand loader rounds the source app logo once, so all canvas uses inherit the same softened corners. Branding does not compete with captions at the top.
- Actual imported word timestamps control progression, including pauses. Without a voiceover alignment file, timing is explicitly estimated. The same explicit timeline drives preview, seeking, frame capture, and export.
- Only category chapters whose narration names at least two distinct metrics in separate spoken cues activate gating. A comparison sentence, single highlight, vague authored focus, or overview does not hide the grid. Unreached tiles retain their bounds as near-neutral, low-saturation placeholders; native content crossfades over 720 ms and its chart follows 140 ms later. Already revealed metrics persist across same-photo/category cuts. This is an original canvas interpretation of the requested generation-preview effect, not copied OpenAI code or an actual loading indicator. Reduced motion preserves speech gating without shimmer or travel.

## Layout refinements

Feature-stage captions use an 80-pixel top inset, with the prepared portrait beginning at 310 pixels. One metric frame calculation now serves portrait and stacked shots: unchanged metric data keeps its bounds and child content across handoffs rather than shrinking or re-revealing. Three-step explainers use the same high caption inset and larger 58-pixel labels, centered between the caption region and footer. Bottom lockups read “Mogging: Face Scan” at a lower baseline; disclosures sit above them. Light stages have a smooth white footer feather. Report captions use a separate stronger 460-pixel, six-level progressive blur and near-opaque light feather to prevent charts competing with the words.

Single-score explainers also use the 80-pixel caption inset. Their category label, rolling score and unit own separate non-overlapping rows in a centered group between caption and footer. The app lockup shares one logical size between its painter, stage placement and metric-card accessory; the icon plus measured name are centered together rather than centering the icon alone.

Every solid-background explainer uses the same 80-pixel caption inset, including statements, comparisons, instruments and sequences with context. The complete caption is measured with its actual font and wrapping, capped at 460 pixels; the visual group centers between that measured block and the footer safe area. Context visuals sit below the caption, not above it. Numeric `/ 10` comparisons reuse the actual rounded mobile report-card painter, including its half-arcs or sliders, typography and value animation. Three scores use a full-width primary card over two smaller cards; two scores use a paired bento. Up to six cards fit uniformly into the available space without an empty fourth cell for three metrics. Non-score counts and conceptual items retain their own semantics rather than receiving invented ratings.

One shared content-placement pass centers the complete visual group vertically and horizontally in the caption-to-footer space, fitting it uniformly inside the safe area. Calendar, ruler, dial, list, context and generic anatomy layouts no longer own competing page-level offsets. Registered photo-to-anatomy motion remains a separate intentional photo handoff. Scale variants include a linear graduated ruler, a curved dashboard ruler with feathered ticks, and a rounded segmented dashboard gauge. Moving calibration values and a fixed index read the same authored score and explicit timeline; no variant invents new measurements.
- Text-only metrics with an explicit grade use the native quality rail; numeric quality scores use the native half-arc. Genuinely unscored descriptions remain compact and unboxed rather than inventing a score. The closing stage uses two native half-arcs in one readable score panel over an enlarged portrait. One smooth alpha feather replaces stacked blurred portrait copies; automatic website subtitles are removed, including legacy imported website-only subtitles.
- Reduced motion removes travel, lift, blur, and word-fill animation while retaining the text and current reading position.

## Related layout changes

Scored comparisons use native report-card bentos, not invented product cards or loose duplicated statistics. Sequence steps remain larger numbered editorial rows, not empty product-feature pills. Layout-owned headline and graphic areas do not overlap. Photograph comparisons do not trigger unrelated feature scores, and an abstract mention of a plan does not generate a task list.

Detected eye, brow, nose-base, mouth, and jaw contours use their own source coordinates and move with the image. Missing contours are omitted, not invented; the face detector is not a hairline detector. A single explicit-clock controller changes treatments every 2.4 seconds. Contours grow by path length from an anchor, reverse direction in alternate treatments, and stagger sparse anchors. Local contour-angle illustrations reveal two short rays and an arc in sequence; they are not labeled as measured skeletal angles. No cross-face baseline is drawn. Eye, nose, and cheek emphasis can lead geometry by 320 ms using feathered exposure changes to the actual photo pixels, rather than a colored face-shaped patch. Jaw, mouth, skin and overall views do not activate that lighting. This is image-space exposure emphasis, not physically reconstructed 3D relighting. The source photograph and contour hierarchy share a persistent named subject node through feature zooms.

Anatomy uses the real CC0 Khronos skull mesh through a lazy Three.js renderer, with physical material, ambient occlusion, key/fill/rim lighting and time-driven camera-relative rotation. It is explicitly labeled as a generic anatomical reference, not a reconstruction of the person. See [asset provenance](creator-anatomy-assets.md).

QOVES' public homepage analysis section was inspected as a visual reference: a large photographic subject, subdued surrounding analytics, fine annotation hierarchy and controlled tonal contrast. These are composition principles, not a claim to reproduce their proprietary analysis, datasets or assets. No demographic, ancestry or clinical inference is borrowed from their marketing material.
