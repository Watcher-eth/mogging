# Modular script and voiceover production

The creator studio’s **Script & voice** tab is the production entry point. The previous example scripts remain as renderer regression fixtures; the studio now uses structured working drafts. These are scaffolds for the next editorial pass, not approved campaign copy or transcriptions of reference videos.

## Author once, cut many ways

A master is a `VideoScript` with one complete thought per cue. Each cue owns its stable section ID, role, title, priority, required preceding sections, narration, performance direction, pause and ranked visual treatments. Text stays in this one script owner; visuals and audio project it.

The master is at most 75 seconds at the conservative 165-word/minute planning pace, including authored pauses. The 30/45/60/75 controls mean **up to** that duration. Cuts end at whole sections; they are not padded or accelerated to hit a number. Automatic cuts keep the hook, direct CTA, product demonstration and takeaway, then include educational sections by priority and dependency. Manual selections and reorders validate required context, hook/closing placement, length and CTA. Removing an explanation never truncates a sentence.

A direct CTA must start by 20 seconds and estimate to 2–4 seconds, targeting three. The scaffold uses “Want your score? Go to Mogging.com.” Short cuts retain two distinct branded sections; longer ones retain a third contextual mention. Use UI demonstrations to support natural lines such as “In the Mogging app…” rather than interrupting repeatedly with URL cards. Audition the spacing: a validator cannot judge how organic a line sounds.

## Visual contract

Each section ranks supported treatments: portrait, close-up, measurement, side profile, lighting, illustrative comparison, report or timeline grid. A choice includes category, image view, optional precise focus, reason, and preferred recipes. Style-specific choices take priority over general fallbacks. “Try another visual variation” cycles alternatives without changing narration or recording.

Production directions are authoritative: the legacy early-app-proof rule cannot replace an unrelated explanation with a score screen. The CTA/product cue owns the app proof instead. Same-photo runs still use the engine’s shared-image transitions; transitions cannot rewrite content. Missing profile images fall back to available portraits rather than inventing a side view—review the source requirements in the brief. Marlon, Johnson and Clavicular now have front, three-quarter and profile studio sources available. The new Marlon/Johnson files are upscaled exports, not native 4K captures.

For each new script, choose evidence that actually supports its claims. An overlay alone does not establish an accurate metric. Rendered demo values and reversible edits are illustrative, not measurements of the pictured person. A category-specific focus can use an existing `metric:<id>` in the brief. Do not use arbitrary skulls, scores or transformations just to fill a shot.

## Record sections once

Four narrator directions are available: editorial, documentary expert, warm conversational guide and curious storyteller. These are stock narrators, independent of the pictured person. Visual style is independent of narrator style.

“Generate reusable voiceover” calls the existing same-origin speech route once per missing approved section. The cache key includes cue identity, exact words, delivery and narrator. Changing a filter/layout or cut order reuses audio; changing wording, delivery or narrator requests new audio. Generation is sequential for consistent order and supports cancellation; already completed sections are retained. A pending provider request may still incur provider usage after cancellation.

Audio is mono signed 16-bit PCM, 24 kHz. Assembly adds the authored pause once per section, concatenates selected sections in cut order, and uses sample counts for actual section durations. If recognized words match the approved wording, they provide the shared caption/motion clock. If recognition is unavailable or mismatched, retain the audio and clearly mark word timing estimated. The recorded cut is never silently accelerated. Export checks actual runtime, CTA placement and CTA duration. The player and MP4 encoder receive the same selected-cut audio.

The script/recording JSON bundle includes the approved master, reference material, narrator and reusable section PCM with observed word offsets. Download it before closing the studio; in-memory recordings alone do not survive reload. Import a saved bundle from the same person’s project to resume without generating completed sections again. Edited writing/visual briefs can also be imported. Source images remain with the selected person; the recording bundle is not an image project archive.

## Writing from existing videos

Expand **Reference videos & visual direction** to add the real URL, transcript and notes. Paste only a transcript you have actually obtained; a URL is not a transcript. The brief distinguishes notes-only sources from supplied transcripts. It does not fetch, transcribe or invent source narration.

Download the writing/visual brief and refine it with source-led notes: the opening question, first concrete payoff, sentence rhythm, proof at the moment of the claim, how the product appears, and how the ending resolves the hook. Write original narration rather than copying a source’s wording. Make sections independently useful; if a pronoun or comparison requires prior context, declare `dependsOn` or rewrite it. Keep stage directions out of spoken text. Import the revised brief, approve wording, audition section joins and compare visual variants against the identical recording.

## Configuration and verified limits

The existing speech endpoint needs server-only `GEMINI_API_KEY`. Optional measured word alignment needs `GOOGLE_SPEECH_API_KEY` and Cloud Speech-to-Text access. GET `/api/creator/voiceover` reports readiness; neither key was configured during this implementation. No actual generated voice quality or provider response has been auditioned. Provider model/access remains the existing endpoint configuration.

Tests use synthetic PCM and observed-word fixtures to verify all cut/style combinations, stable cue identity, order, duration, invalidation, dependency/CTA gates and bundle restore. They do not establish subjective script or voice quality. ## Script semantics and visual directing

`video-semantics.ts` owns narration meaning, independently of layout: exact quote, target feature, intent, operation, view, confidence and rationale. `video-director.ts` is the sole production visual director. The older treatment keyword scorer and feature-format override have been removed. Script section visual preferences remain useful for category IDs, metric focus and format-specific app proof; they cannot override a spoken edit with an unrelated animation.

Local decoding works offline and conservatively splits complete spoken thoughts. Optional **Script & voice → Visual plan → Refine visual plan with AI** calls `/api/creator/visual-plan` once. It uses server-only `GEMINI_API_KEY`, exact-quote structured output, same-origin/admin checks and rate limiting. No model calls occur during animation or export. This checkout has no configured Gemini key at verification time; provider execution has been tested with a mocked response, not claimed as a successful live generation.

Model output must cover every original section once, quote all words in order and preserve negations. Rewriting narration drops that section's old semantic metadata. Confidence below .7 falls back to an observation instead of manufacturing an edit. Model metadata changes do not invalidate the unchanged section recording. Every visual beat is projected back to the original cue/word identities; actual speech alignment remains the timing authority.

Format grammar ranks only meaning-compatible primitives: portrait, feature crop, drawn callout/leader, anchored measurement, reversible shape edit, registered pair, comparison wipe, four-view expansion, profile outline, lighting, native report and category timeline grid. Seeds select among valid alternatives using subject, exact text, format and variant; adjacent repetition is penalised. A grid keeps the original first image opaque and interpolates its placement into the grid rather than fading the portrait away. The wipe uses the same pupil-registered before/after frame and an eased monotonic reveal. Reduced motion uses a static comparison.

Prepared retouches have explicit `GeneratorImage.retouch` metadata: feature, operation and baseline image ID. Bundled freckles and beard assets mean **add**, never remove or replace. The upload control asks what the edited copy changes and registers it against the unchanged original front portrait. Ear swaps and hair restyling use matching edited photographs; geometric warps must not pretend to replace ears or hair. Missing evidence is listed in the visual plan and falls back to the original. Registered comparisons preserve the original wardrobe in either theme; other portraits retain dark wardrobe variants.

The director does not promise infinite unique edits from finite assets, automatic arbitrary Photoshop retouching, or QOVES-equivalent final quality without editorial review. Those need source-specific retouches and auditioned videos with the final voiceover. Existing native anatomy, calendar and other utility slides remain available in the custom library; they are not inserted when the voiceover does not call for them. Scores and potential retain direct value animation only, without highlight boxes.


### Continuous portrait action programs

Portrait production and the legacy script compiler now attach `portraitProgram` to each portrait beat. One engine (`portrait-program.ts`) owns its schema, semantic guide selection, preparation and drawing. Four spoken words form an action interval (about 1.45 seconds at the default pace). The actual measured word offsets replace estimates whenever a recording is aligned. Each interval continues drawing, morphing, revealing markers or developing a callout; it is not an overlay reveal followed by a several-second hold. Reduced motion resolves directly to the current spoken evidence.

The program selects among the 43 registered metric guides, philtrum/skin callouts, anatomical light planes and visible-side profile paths. Explicit metric names constrain selection to that metric; lighting uses planes rather than unrelated ratios. Generic passages can explore compatible guides and treatments. A sentence can hand attention from cheek to jaw, while phrases such as “keep the eyes fixed” remain references instead of new targets. Missing detected paths are omitted rather than drawing fabricated anatomy. Ear replacement remains a registered photo-asset operation; it is not synthesized from cheek landmarks.

Each shot has one active geometry owner. Successive actions morph a single sampled geometry instead of stacking face maps. Photo warps carry their outlines through the same displacement. Feature-study edits now develop through most of their beat instead of reaching full strength immediately and holding. Manual custom templates clear a stale automatic program when the user selects another overlay/layout.

Lighting no longer derives fake surface normals, a head-shaped light field, or selective desaturation. Its neutral exposure fields cover the whole subject consistently, including neck and shoulders. Side, soft-front, spot and rim states are bounded in exposure; soft-front returns to near-original illumination. Shared-image handoffs blend exposure fields, not differently shaded photographic layers. This is an editorial lighting illustration, not reconstructed physical 3D relighting. Shader compilation and initial material upload occur during preparation, before the playback clock starts.

The local decoder separates meaningful contrasting clauses (“side lighting …; softer frontal …”, “side view … while three-quarter …”) while retaining exact original words and cue ownership. Moving a modular section between sections moves all of its visual beats together, preserving reusable section audio; ordinary single-clip reordering retains its existing behavior.

Explicit feature words add action anchors between cadence intervals; focus changes occur on the corresponding measured word. A general script does not inherit the topic dropdown as an unstated claim: topic context must be present in its hook. Turned portraits use visible contour programs rather than frontal spacing/width instruments. Ear notes are omitted without dedicated ear geometry instead of placing a guessed circle on the cheek.

Curved anatomical paths reuse the bounded contour interpolation from the face UI; straight measurement instruments remain straight. The effect shader now applies all four control regions used by the feature tools (previously only the first two reached the GPU), keeping mouth, paired-feature and brow/jaw edits consistent with their CPU-projected outlines.

Overlay pacing uses six-word planning slots and a 1.8-second minimum dwell on the recorded word clock; tails shorter than 1.5 seconds are absorbed into the preceding guide. Dotted, dashed and frosted solid strokes share one renderer. New instruments draw from a point; only nearby contours in the same anatomical family and treatment may morph. Short shots retain one overlay for their available duration.

Editorial annotations quote descriptive phrases from the original narration and retain the keyword word index. Regular-weight monochrome text reserves its complete line layout before per-character opacity reveals; aligned audio controls when the label begins. Brow silhouettes, brow-to-upper-lid brackets, lip-height brackets and nose-axis guides use registered landmarks. Compact specimen panels redraw the actual contours, including both brows; missing contours are omitted. Descriptive feature beats prefer close-ups, detail crops use their anatomical guide rather than the full-face map, and the opening feature-study detail moves from its whole-portrait frame before the guide draws. Existing persistent-image framing owns all camera continuity.

Face overlay drawing bypasses UI theme inversion in both themes. Dark-mode legacy face/profile guides also force light ink. Annotation labels remain white wherever their reserved frame intersects the detected face boundary, including grayscale skin highlights. Elsewhere, a reused 40×12 rendered-canvas sample selects dark ink only for predominantly neutral near-white space; dark, coloured, mixed or unavailable samples keep white with a subtle contrast shadow. This changes ink only, without a label background panel or typewriter reflow.

Compatible caption layouts and bottom backdrops are rendered once after the content transition, so directional pushes or restrained zooms affect only underlying scenes. Compatibility includes caption edge, type size, line height and backdrop role; top-to-bottom caption changes retain authored scene transitions. Same-image push/wipe/reframe/reveal/ripple handoffs use the existing single-portrait shared-element interpolation. Feature-study image choreography retains its own geometry handoff and now rolls matching captions without replaying text entrance tracks. Content transitions use deterministic direction/zoom choices, bounded easing, theme-correct exposed canvas colours, and immediate reduced-motion rendering.

Equivalent anatomical views now form the variation pool for named brow shape/arch, brow-to-lid spacing, eye shape and jaw-outline narration. They include paired/single-side outlines, upper brow edges, eye-plus-brow context, and left/right jaw contours; exact ratios remain on their named metric. A contour panel is an independent treatment, rather than an inevitable consequence of selecting a paired brow. The first prepared guide owns the scene's precise crop, so a single-side variant gets a matching close-up without camera jumps at each subsequent overlay cue. Feature breakdown and single-photo feature studies fill the lower portrait stage with available source pixels, preserving aspect ratio and exposing torso rather than terminating the image early.

CMYK portrait filters preserve more original skin tone by making the paper between ink marks partially transparent (Color print .68; Coral risograph and Vintage press .65). Ink colours, dot texture, portrait mask and layout are unchanged. Ink press uses Paper's CMYK ink-shape mode with a restrained neutral/warm palette and .3 paper alpha; Editorial dither uses Paper's image-dithering shader with original colours, six colour steps and a fine 8×8 ordered pattern. Both are prepared once and available through the shared Filters catalog.

Fine paper now alternates prominently with colour print across eligible portrait runs and uses stronger roughness/fibre at modest paper blending. Printed portraits partially recover source hue/saturation through a colour blend while keeping ink luminance texture; the final source-atop pass preserves the original cutout alpha exactly. Prepared programs include a cached 64×96 subject-alpha map. Annotation candidates are scored for clear background or consistent subject coverage so labels avoid mixed neck/shoulder edges before ink is selected. Same-image print changes sweep between already-prepared registered images with a curved, lightly bristled clip; caption layers and face geometry remain under their existing shared-element owners, and reduced motion resolves immediately.
