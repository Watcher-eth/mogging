What is happening

The strongest measured problem is payment conversion, followed by weak recurring value. The largest upstream uncertainty is whether creator views generate buying intent. We can see substantial reach and a working path into the app, but very few people become paying customers and many subscribers immediately switch off renewal.

October 6–November 4, 2026: 18,947,749 reported cumulative views on 230 submissions created in the period; approximately $497.95 in sales; $5,160.91 in recorded creator payments. These are different accounting cohorts, so this is a period-level economic diagnostic, not attributed campaign ROAS.

There is no defensible end-to-end “video view → customer” conversion rate yet. Views repeat, views accumulate after publication, people search for the brand, links lose referrers, and web browser IDs are not automatically the same as app installation IDs. The scale of the gap is real, but an exact percentage of viewers lost on the video would be invented.

Linked attempt tracking covers October 17 onward and is not the entire month. All 49 purchased attempts in this tracked subset have an activation outcome; 45 also have a direct matching production billing fact in this query. This does not establish that all historical payments reconciled perfectly.

137 of 1,162 mobile purchase-start events lack an attempt ID. Another 100 linked attempts have no terminal outcome. Those are observability gaps, not proof that customers were charged and lost access.

198 of 259 recorded cancellation responses (76.4%) cited price; 40 said not ready and 8 payment trouble. This is a self-selected response sample. Recorded cancellations are often fast (median 4.3 seconds), consistent with friction at the store confirmation or commitment step, but not proof of why each customer cancelled. The follow-up offer has 51 tracked attempts and zero tracked successful purchases: test its eligibility, actual price and attribution before treating it as effective.

Confirmed tracked failures: 13 purchase-not-allowed, 10 store-problem, 1 offline product-loading error. These deserve fixes, but eliminating 24 failures cannot by itself repair 850 cancellations.

The web authentication-error page was seen by 754 browser identities; 621 later had an identity-link or checkout event. It needs investigation, but 754 is not the number of permanently lost customers.

Active access is not the same as intent to renew. Apple reports 36 active paid plans; the ledger shows many subscriptions with renewal disabled but time remaining. Do not forecast recurring revenue from active-access counts alone.

Among the 48 new subscribers, 4/13 who never scheduled cancellation completed a protocol task, versus 7/35 who did. The sample is small, exposure time differs and selection bias is substantial; this is a useful hypothesis about recurring value, not causal proof.

Nine mobile devices logged an evaluation failure and only two subsequently logged completion. Failures include analysis/request failures and credit/payment precondition errors (HTTP 402), so this is not a clean provider-outage rate. Link payment, entitlement, upload, reservation and result IDs to establish the seven unresolved cases.

What to optimize next

  1. Make the numbers trustworthy and repair confirmed friction. Reconcile verified charges to access and completed results. Resolve unresolved attempts and authentication errors. Check live prices and offer eligibility per release. Do this before increasing spend.
  2. Match the purchase to the visitor’s intent. Test a clear individual evaluation alongside Pro, transparent localized pricing before the store sheet, and a concrete explanation of what keeps being useful after day one. Preserve a useful preview without implying payment has already generated an evaluation.
  3. Design the first return. After the first report, lead to one actionable protocol task, a saved baseline and a meaningful next check-in. Measure D1/D7 paid-user return, task completion, repeat evaluation and actual renewal. Do not optimize notification opens alone.
  4. Buy intent, not view totals. Keep controlled creator experiments, but require identifiable CTAs and tagged destinations. Compare creatives that demonstrate the product and ongoing value against entertainment-only formats. Judge creators on verified first purchases, activated customers and 30/60-day contribution, with views as context.
  5. Evaluate web and native separately, then join verified business totals. Native StoreKit and Stripe have different checkout mechanics. Keep their funnels separate; combine deduplicated customers, sales, refunds and contribution only at the business layer.

Doubling the mobile paywall conversion rate would be useful, but roughly 50 additional first purchases at current traffic does not automatically close a $4,663 period cash gap. The improvement needs to compound across qualified traffic, conversion, recurring use and acquisition cost.

The dashboard we should run the business from

One economic overview: gross sales, refunds, developer proceeds, creator accrued cost, creator cash cost, variable processing/inference cost, contribution, new paying customers, renewal revenue, renewing MRR, non-renewing active access and cohort payback. Separate estimated from provider-confirmed values.

Two conversion funnels: web exposure → photo selection → authentication → checkout created → verified payment → entitlement → completed evaluation → viewed report; native first open → onboarding → authentication → photo → localized products ready → store attempt → verified transaction → activation → report. Show both user and attempt counts, and unknown outcomes.

One acquisition table: creator, post, campaign, platform, daily view increment, tagged visits, attributed installs, first verified purchases, 30/60-day proceeds, fully allocated cost and contribution. Keep unattributed revenue visible rather than distributing it arbitrarily.

One cohort view: install/purchase week × release × source × plan, with D1/D7/D30 return, report completion, task completion, repeat evaluation, cancellation timing, first renewal and 30/60/90-day realized value. Show immature cells and small samples explicitly.

One trust panel: last provider sync, ledger variance, FX completeness, identity/attribution match rate, missing attempt outcomes and payment-to-access/result exceptions. Success is trustworthy business outcomes, not an ever-growing event list.

Sources and limits

Read-only production Postgres aggregates; direct authenticated App Store Connect analytics and sales queries; deployed configuration metadata; tracking and billing source review. Known internal accounts, linked internal device identities, sandbox sessions and non-production runtimes were excluded from customer event aggregates. Existing experiment-creator exclusions were used for the main creator cohort. All 230 cohort submission URLs are distinct.

Production event data continues arriving after period close; event occurrence time defines the window, while late receipts already present at audit time are included. Creator view and approval fields are current record snapshots, so later rereviews can change them. Only 6/230 posts have a published-at field. OCR assessments exist for 54 submissions and begin November 2; none of the cohort rows supplies the specific verified-totalViews flag used by the existing performance query. This is not a claim that the OCR pipeline is absent.

Actual Stripe webhook receipts and RevenueCat webhook receipts inspected are processed. The 1,178 unprocessed receipt rows use the older stripe_reconciliation namespace; they are not evidence of 1,178 lost real payments. Current Stripe reconciliation has a recent watermark.

Apple aggregates are USD estimates and use Apple daily reporting boundaries. Their sales and proceeds are not two sources of revenue. Apple usage retention is opt-in and should not be compared as an identical population to first-party retention. No causal attribution, unique-viewer CTR, fully reconciled cross-provider lifetime value or statistically significant test winner is asserted.
